\section{Discussion}
\subsection{What the CuratedTREC Correction Changes}
CuratedTREC uses regular-expression answer references in the official DPR data. Evaluating them as literal strings undercounted answer-bearing passages and made the corpus appear unusually sparse for this test set. With the official regex evaluator, 87.75\% of CuratedTREC questions have a matching answer expression somewhere in the corpus, the highest coverage among the five tests. The earlier explanation that CuratedTREC had low absolute accuracy mainly because its answer passages were absent is therefore withdrawn.

The corrected first-stage results show that DPR Multi reaches Hit@20 of 51.15\% on CuratedTREC and the local BM25+DPR fusion reaches 52.16\%. Reader reranking reaches 55.62\% at Hit@20, a 4.47-point increase over DPR Multi. Its Hit@100 remains 68.01\%, equal to the DPR candidate set's coverage at that cutoff: the Reader reorders the 100 passages but cannot retrieve a passage outside them. Among covered questions, DPR Multi and BM25+DPR reach 58.29\% and 59.44\% at Hit@20, respectively. Coverage-conditioned values describe ranking after answer availability is accounted for; they do not replace full-test accuracy or establish semantic support.

\subsection{Reader Interpretation}
On TriviaQA, the Reader raises Hit@20 from 33.60\% to 41.35\%; Hit@100 is effectively unchanged (48.04\% versus 48.06\%). The answer-span head achieves 12.54\% normalized Exact Match on test, while 48.06\% of questions have an answer-matching passage among the 100 reranked candidates. This gap reflects the difference between locating an answer-bearing passage and predicting its exact answer span. The experiment uses the released Reader checkpoint and evaluates inference; it does not train a new Reader.
