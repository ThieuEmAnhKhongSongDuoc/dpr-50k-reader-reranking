\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Lexical and Dense Retrieval}
BM25 ranks passages from lexical evidence with term-frequency saturation and document-length normalization~\cite{bm25}. It remains a useful reference because lexical overlap can identify specific entities and expressions that a dense model may rank poorly. Our lexical reference uses Apache Lucene, with implementation details stated separately from those of the DPR paper.

DPR encodes a question and a passage independently with BERT-based encoders~\cite{dpr,bert}. Passage representations can be computed once and reused across questions. The independence of the two encoders enables efficient vector search, while a fixed passage vector must support many potential questions. In our experiments the released weights are held fixed; the original contrastive training objective explains the model's provenance rather than describing a training run performed here.

\subsection{Vector Search}
FAISS provides methods for exact and approximate similarity search~\cite{faiss}. HNSW organizes vectors in a graph to obtain approximate nearest neighbors~\cite{hnsw}. The DPR paper reports an HNSW deployment for its large corpus. At 50,000 passages, exact inner-product search is feasible and avoids approximate-search errors relative to the DPR scoring function. Exactness of vector search is distinct from answer relevance: a correctly retrieved highest-scoring vector can still correspond to an unhelpful passage.

\subsection{Neural Reranking and Readers}
BERT-based reranking is an established way to score a query and a passage jointly~\cite{nogueira,bert}. Sequence-to-sequence relevance scoring is explored by monoT5~\cite{monot5}; token-level late interaction is used by ColBERT and its later ColBERTv2 version~\cite{colbert,colbertv2}; and RankGPT studies listwise LLM reranking~\cite{rankgpt}. We evaluate one released MS MARCO cross-encoder as a frozen inference baseline and do not train it. The original DPR system also provides an extractive reader with passage-selection and answer-span heads~\cite{dprcode}. We reuse its passage-selection head as a reranker and report its span exact-match metric separately where available. The cross-encoder only reorders retrieved passages and does not generate answer spans.

Natural Questions and TriviaQA supply complementary question-answering benchmarks~\cite{nq,trivia}. We use the official DPR-formatted test resources, including the CuratedTREC, WebQuestions, and SQuAD resources distributed with that repository, rather than creating a random train--validation--test split of Wikipedia passages.
