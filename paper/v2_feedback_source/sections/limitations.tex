\section{Limitations}
The evaluation uses one fixed reservoir sample of 50,000 passages from the 21-million-passage Wikipedia corpus. Sampling changes both answer availability and the distribution of distractors. The measured coverage is an answer-string availability diagnostic; it is not a semantic relevance judgment, and a matching answer string can occur in a passage that does not support the answer to the question.

All DPR and Reader weights are released author checkpoints. The experiments evaluate inference and do not reproduce the DPR training schedule or update model weights. The DPR Reader relevance head is used to reorder retrieved candidates; answer-span Exact Match is a separate metric and is reported only for datasets whose answer references are literal answer strings.

The principal comparisons use one checkpoint, one corpus sample, and one run per configuration. They do not estimate variability across corpus seeds or establish statistical significance. Per-query outputs are retained for paired analyses, but such tests are not reported unless they are computed and documented separately.

IndexFlatIP gives exact inner-product results for the supplied vectors. The study does not compare HNSW and FlatIP under a matched runtime/recall budget, and an exact vector index does not guarantee that a passage is relevant or contains a supported answer.

The Reader is evaluated on a finite candidate set produced by DPR. It cannot recover answer-bearing passages outside that set. Its answer-span predictions are also affected by the model's input truncation and by the answer-string evaluation convention. These results should therefore be interpreted as retrieval and reranking measurements, not as a complete open-domain QA system evaluation.
