\begin{abstract}
We study Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) inference on a fixed reservoir sample of 50,000 Wikipedia passages. Full-test lexical, dense, and hybrid references cover five QA benchmarks and 28,219 questions. Using DPR's official regular-expression evaluator, CuratedTREC answer-match coverage is 87.75\%, and coverage across the five tests is 65.68\%. After passage-ID normalization, local BM25+DPR score fusion slightly exceeds DPR Multi at Hit@20 and Hit@100 on all five tests. We evaluate the official extractive Reader at $K=100$ in FP16 after running TriviaQA development. Reader reranking raises CuratedTREC Hit@20 from 51.15\% to 55.62\% and TriviaQA Hit@20 from 33.60\% to 41.35\%. A frozen MS MARCO cross-encoder also reorders the same 100 candidates, with smaller Hit@20 gains over DPR ordering and lower accuracy than the Reader. TriviaQA Reader span Exact Match is 12.54\% on test. Per-question candidate IDs, relevance scores, and span predictions are retained. These are inference results on a reduced corpus and are not directly comparable to the original 21-million-passage benchmark.
\end{abstract}
